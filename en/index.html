Why directional drilling instead of a trench

Underground work no longer means a torn-up yard and a closed road. Here is what you actually gain with horizontal directional drilling.

  • The road stays intact

    We don’t cut the asphalt or lift the paving. The pipe goes underneath, and everything on top stays exactly as it was: no holes to fill and no repair bill left behind.

  • Fast: hours, not days

    A crossing that would take days to dig is usually drilled in a few hours. The street stays open, neighbours don’t need a detour, and you’re back on schedule quickly.

  • Cost-effective in the long run

    You don’t pay to rebuild the road, the fence or the lawn. The price of the job is effectively the final price, with no hidden costs once the rig has left.

  • Clean, no mess

    We work from two small pits, one at each end. No mounds of soil in the yard, no mud on the wheels, no dust in the house.

  • Made for roads and pavements

    Directional drilling is built for places where you can’t dig: carriageways, pavements, junctions, paved yards and plots with buildings on top.

  • Centimetre accuracy

    The drill head is steered from the surface along the whole route. We always know where it is, avoid existing utilities and come out exactly at the agreed point.

How we work: 3 stages

A clear process where every stage is visible and controlled.

  1. 01

    Setting the underground route

    We plan the pipe's path under the obstacle and drill a pilot bore, steered from the surface.

  2. 02

    Sizing the bore for the pipe

    We enlarge the drilled channel to the right diameter for the pipe or protective duct.

  3. 03

    Precise final installation

    We pull the pipe through the prepared channel and leave it ready to connect at both ends.

Frequently asked questions

What is horizontal directional drilling (HDD)?
It’s a trenchless way to install pipes and cables underground. A specialised rig drills a curved channel under the obstacle, steered from the surface, and the pipe is then pulled through it. Nothing is dug on top.
Will the road, yard or fence be damaged?
No. We only work from two small pits at the entry and exit of the route. The asphalt, paving, lawn, fence or foundation above stays untouched.
How long does a job take?
It depends on the length and the ground, but a typical road crossing is usually done in a few hours. Dug by hand or excavator, the same job can take days.
Which utilities can you install trenchless?
Water, sewer, gas, power cables and internet (fibre). We carry out crossings under roads, pavements, fences and foundations.
